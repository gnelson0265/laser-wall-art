# Aesthetic refinement brief

The approved five-layer set remains the geometric baseline. Refine a new master
before deriving new layers; never smooth or redraw successive layers separately.

## Findings from the approved ginkgo

- The source established the desired fan silhouette, notch, and radiating scrolls.
- Skeleton reinforcement introduces small bumps where delicate source curves meet
  broad bands. A clean master should have smooth curves and bridges wide enough
  for manufacturing before raster preparation.
- Several subtraction junctions end in blunt or awkward stubs. Removable features
  should terminate at deliberate branch junctions with smooth surviving contours.
- The visible area by ply is currently 4.6%, 3.9%, 55.0%, 17.1%, and 19.4% from
  bottom to top. Layer 3 dominates the assembled green image. This passes current
  geometric checks but is not a balanced visual progression.

## Next master criteria

Preserve the broad, recognizable ginkgo fan and integrated radial vein structure.
Use smooth, continuous outer curves; a deliberate central notch; tangent scroll
junctions; spacious teardrop openings; and a hierarchy of large, medium, and small
ornaments. Vary curl sizes while keeping coherent spacing. Keep the petiole
proportional to the fan. Avoid pixel-scale bumps, skinny spikes, crowded curl
centers, and repeated isolated-looking motifs.

Draw load-bearing material generously above the 38-pixel minimum at final size,
so manufacturing preparation requires only modest correction. Keep an exact
mirrored construction in the validated master. Image-generator symmetry and
connectivity are concept guidance, never evidence of manufacturing compliance.

## Review and manufacture

1. Review one black-on-white master concept for shape, flow, spacing, and detail.
2. Construct or clean that master once and enforce exact symmetry and width.
3. Decompose it into deliberately removable complete features at clean junctions.
4. Schedule removals using exposed area and feature importance, aiming for visibly
   meaningful contributions from all five plies. Do not force equal area if it
   weakens the composition or the minimum material widths.
5. Generate every layer through subtraction of the same original components.
6. Check all geometric invariants and show the aligned green stack before export.

The next image is an aesthetic concept, not a validated cutting file. No existing
approved manufacturing layer is modified by this design exploration.


## User-directed line-flow rules

The eye should follow a thick primary vein continuously into smaller secondary
branches and progressively finer ornamental lines. Use smooth tangents and
gradual width transitions at every organic junction. Prefer flowing curves with
smoothly changing curvature over short bends, beads, abrupt cutoffs, or sudden
changes of weight. Start with a primary:secondary:fine width ratio around
3:1.8:1, adjusted for composition. The narrowest retained material still has to
meet the 38-pixel manufacturing minimum; a concept's hairline tip is not a valid
production feature.

For an explicitly constructed golden logarithmic spiral, use
`r(theta) = a * exp((2 * ln(phi) / pi) * theta)`, where
`phi = (1 + sqrt(5)) / 2`. Radius grows by phi per quarter turn. Use this where it
serves the composition, with smooth tangential transitions into the structural
vein. Do not force every curve to be a golden spiral. Image-generation references
to the golden mean are art direction, not proof of this geometric relationship.

Hard edges and corners remain when they identify the subject: triangles keep
vertices, buildings keep architectural edges, and other angular subjects retain
their defining geometry. Mark these intentionally rather than smoothing them
indiscriminately. Organic subjects such as this ginkgo use smooth flowing curves.
